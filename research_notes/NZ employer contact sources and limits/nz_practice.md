# NZ practice: finding and contacting the hiring manager or HR contact

Scope: what NZ recruiters, employers and career services say about direct outreach after applying online, and what backfires. Current as of 2024-2026 where the source is dated; older sources are flagged. Method note: this is based on web search results and a small number of page fetches. SEEK and LinkedIn were NOT fetched (per constraint); SEEK claims below come from search-result excerpts of SEEK NZ career-advice articles and should be verified on the live pages before being quoted. Two fetches failed (Auckland University careers page returned 404; Robert Walters networking page returned 403), so the findings rely on search snippets for those sources.

## Do NZ recruiters and employers welcome direct contact when an ad says "apply online"?

### Takeaway
Direct contact is accepted in NZ when it is one short, polite, role-specific message to a named or clearly identified contact, sent after the formal application and after a sensible wait. It backfires when it is repeated, spread across many channels, sent to people who have asked for no contact, or used where the ad or process forbids it. No NZ source found explicitly addresses "no agencies" or "SEEK only" wording.

### Cited Findings
- SEEK NZ says the contact name often appears in the job ad or in the automated email received after applying, and that direct messages matter most when experience is thin or competition is heavy. Source: [SEEK NZ, "How to follow up on a job application"](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-follow-up-a-job-application) (search excerpt, not fetched).
- Same SEEK NZ article: candidates often skip follow-up because it feels annoying, but a follow-up lets you restate fit, which can matter if automated filters screened you out. Source: same URL (search excerpt).
- Enhancv warns that in formal recruitment processes, unsolicited follow-up can look unprofessional, and that the listing may say not to follow up; be respectful if it does. Source: [Enhancv, "Should you call a job after applying?"](https://enhancv.com/blog/should-you-call-a-job-after-applying/) (international, not NZ-specific; search excerpt).
- Ask a Manager notes the "always go around HR" advice is overstated because most applicants do not stand out to the hiring manager. Source: [Ask a Manager, "should I go around HR and apply with the hiring manager directly"](https://askamanager.org/2014/06/should-i-go-around-hr-and-apply-with-the-hiring-manager-directly.html) (US, 2014, dated; search excerpt).
- Immigration NZ states that people also contact employers directly, and that this is often the most successful approach. Source: [Immigration NZ, "How to find a job in New Zealand"](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/finding-work-in-new-zealand/how-to-find-a-job-in-new-zealand) (search excerpt).
- Unitec careers guidance suggests phoning a company directly to explore unadvertised opportunities and persevering even if it feels hard at first. Source: [Unitec, "What to say when job hunting" (PDF)](https://unitec.ac.nz/sites/default/files/public/documents/What%20to%20say%20when%20job%20hunting.pdf) (undated; search excerpt).
- A Robert Half NZ survey found 57% of Kiwi workers would lose interest in a role if they did not hear back within 10 business days of applying. Source: [Robert Half NZ press release](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/about/press/how-to-lose-a-candidate-in-10-business-days) (search excerpt).
- NZ Herald jobseeker advice cautions against approaching a hiring manager through six or seven different channels, or applying to multiple roles at one company, and suggests treating the hiring manager as a potential business partner rather than a gatekeeper. Source: [NZ Herald, "Jobseekers need to address trust issues"](https://nzherald.co.nz/nz/jobseekers-need-to-address-trust-issues/7335L4QW6COVGBQLDILCYLUPEM) (search excerpt; date not confirmed).
- Applywave's NZ Post page recommends applying first, then emailing the recruiter who handles the relevant department and referencing the specific role. Source: [Applywave, "How to contact recruiters at NZ Post"](https://applywave.app/recruiter-contact/nz-post) (third-party vendor page; treat as low authority). Applywave's other NZ company pages appeared to be generic templates and were not relied on.
- Trade Me Jobs's advice on recruiters: choose a recruiter specialising in your field, be candid about what you want, and respond quickly. Source: [Trade Me Jobs, "Working with recruiters when job hunting in NZ"](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/working-with-recruiters-when-job-hunting-in-nz) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- The consistent NZ advice is: apply through the official channel first, then make one targeted follow-up. Outreach without a prior application is weaker and more likely to be read as going around process.
- For an ad that says "no agencies" or "applications through SEEK only", the most defensible reading (inference, not stated by any source found) is that the employer is hiring directly and wants applications through its stated route. Contacting a recruiter would be out of place; a single follow-up to the contact named in the ad or confirmation email is the most that seems appropriate, and only if the ad does not say not to follow up.
- Where an ad explicitly says "apply online only" or "no contact", treat that as a stop signal.

### Gaps
- No NZ source found that directly addresses "no agencies" wording or what it means for direct contact.
- No NZ-specific survey found on how hiring managers view unsolicited contact after applying.

## Recommended ways to identify the right person

### Takeaway
The sources agree on a short list: the contact named in the ad or the auto-reply email, then the company's team or leadership pages, then reception or HR by phone, then a recruiter who works in that function. Manual professional-networking searches are common advice, but no NZ source found addresses LinkedIn specifically beyond general networking guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Contact name in the ad or application confirmation email: [SEEK NZ, "How to follow up on a job application"](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-follow-up-a-job-application) (search excerpt).
- If no contact is given, call reception and ask who to address: same SEEK NZ article (search excerpt).
- Choose a recruiter who specialises in your field; good recruiters have strong contacts in the market; ask people in your line of work for referrals: [careers.govt.nz, "How to work with a recruiter to find a job"](https://careers.govt.nz/articles/how-to-work-with-a-recruiter-to-find-a-job) and [Trade Me Jobs](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/working-with-recruiters-when-job-hunting-in-nz) (search excerpts).
- Hidden job market: a Tertiary Education Commission resource says employers often recruit through networking with people they know, and estimates the hidden share at around 70%. Source: [TEC hidden job market interactive (PDF)](https://cnzweb.cwp.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/hidden-job-market-interactive.pdf) (search excerpt; estimates vary; an NZ Herald piece cites around 80%, so treat both as rough).
- Hays NZ advises approaching people first for advice rather than for a job, then keeping in touch. Source: [Hays NZ, "How to find the hidden job market"](https://hays.net.nz/career-advice/job-hunting/hidden-jobs-market) (search excerpt; date not confirmed).
- University of Auckland careers service frames networking as asking for advice and information, and recommends a three-to-four-sentence summary of who you are and what you want. Source: [University of Auckland, "Finding and approaching employers"](https://www.auckland.ac.nz/en/study/student-support/career-development-and-employability-services/networking-find-job/finding-approaching-employers.html) (search excerpt only; the fetch returned 404).
- Robert Half NZ 2024 research: 31% of workers who felt ghosted said they would request updates from the hiring manager they first connected with. Source: [Robert Half NZ press release](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/about/press/how-to-lose-a-candidate-in-10-business-days) (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- A manual, role-specific search of the company's own team pages and a professional-network profile for people already in that team is in line with NZ advice. Automated bulk harvesting of staff contact details is not described as acceptable practice by any source found, and it runs against the privacy point below.

### Gaps
- No NZ source found that explicitly recommends or discourages manual LinkedIn searching for hiring-manager identification (LinkedIn was not fetched).
- No NZ source found on whether reception calls to ask "who should I address" are welcomed or disliked by employers.

## Etiquette and timing for follow-up in NZ

### Takeaway
Wait about one to two weeks, or until any decision date the employer gave. Send one short email, then at most one more follow-up, and then stop. Email is the usual first channel; a phone call is accepted, and some NZ survey data suggests many people prefer phone.

### Cited Findings
- SEEK NZ (recruiter quoted): a default of about a week after applying, unless the employer gave a different timeline. Source: [SEEK NZ, "How to follow up on a job application"](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-follow-up-a-job-application) (search excerpt).
- SEEK NZ follow-up email guide: best sent about two weeks after applying; if nothing, wait another week or two before a final follow-up. Source: [SEEK NZ, "How to write a follow up email for a job application"](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-write-a-follow-up-email-for-a-job-application-templates-and-tips) (search excerpt).
- SEEK NZ: follow up twice; if no response, it may just not be the right time. Source: [SEEK NZ, "How to follow up on a job application"](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-follow-up-a-job-application) (search excerpt).
- SEEK NZ: email is suggested first because it lets the recipient respond when convenient; a phone call is reasonable afterwards; about half of NZ survey respondents said they prefer phone. Source: SEEK NZ article as above (search excerpt; the survey figure is as quoted by SEEK and not independently checked).
- Robert Walters NZ (after an interview): if a decision timeline was given, wait until after it; otherwise two to three business days is ideal. Source: [Robert Walters NZ, "How to follow up after a job interview"](https://www.robertwalters.co.nz/insights/career-advice/blog/how-to-follow-up-after-a-job-interview.html) (search excerpt; this is interview follow-up, not application follow-up).
- Another source suggests waiting 5-7 business days and sending one follow-up email, then logging it. Source: [New Zealand Shores, "Should I follow up on a job application?"](https://www.newzealandshores.com/work-in-new-zealand/job-search-support/follow-up-job-applications/) (search excerpt; authority not checked).
- Robert Half NZ: most jobseekers give employers up to a month to respond, and many lose interest sooner. Source: [Robert Half NZ press release](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/about/press/how-to-lose-a-candidate-in-10-business-days) (search excerpt).
- SEEK NZ: a follow-up after an application can restate fit; keep it short and professional. Source: SEEK NZ article as above (search excerpt).

### Inferences
- A reasonable NZ default is: one follow-up email after 5-10 business days (or after any stated decision date and any closing-date rule), one further follow-up after another week, then stop. Phone follow-up is acceptable as an alternative to the second email, not in addition to it on the same day.
- Note the closing date on the ad before following up, so you do not chase too early.

### Gaps
- Sources disagree on the exact window (about a week vs two weeks). No single authoritative NZ source found.
- No NZ source found on how many calls are acceptable; the "two follow-ups" guidance is SEEK's, not a survey finding.

## Personal channels: mobile, WhatsApp, personal social media

### Takeaway
No NZ source found addresses WhatsApp or personal social media for contacting hiring managers. The closest advice is that multi-channel chasing is seen as pushy, and a single professional channel (email, or a call to the main line) is the safer default. Treat personal channels as off-limits unless the employer or recruiter has offered them.

### Cited Findings
- NZ Herald: a hiring manager does not want to hear you have approached them through six or seven different channels. Source: [NZ Herald, "Jobseekers need to address trust issues"](https://nzherald.co.nz/nz/jobseekers-need-to-address-trust-issues/7335L4QW6COVGBQLDILCYLUPEM) (search excerpt).
- Robert Walters NZ offers guidance on responding to recruiters on LinkedIn, but the snippet found did not address hiring-manager contact on personal channels. Source: [Robert Walters NZ, "How to respond to recruiters on LinkedIn"](https://www.robertwalters.co.nz/insights/career-advice/blog/how-to-respond-to-recruiters-on-linkedin.html) (not fetched; title only).

### Inferences
- Using a candidate's personal mobile or personal social accounts to reach a hiring manager, without that person having offered them, is likely to be read as overstepping, and is also the kind of contact data an app should not be harvesting.

### Gaps
- No NZ source on WhatsApp use in recruitment. This is a genuine gap; do not present any WhatsApp norm as sourced.

## NZ-specific norms

### Takeaway
NZ is a small, relationship-driven market, and networking is widely recommended. Public sector roles are advertised on jobs.govt.nz and each vacancy's own instructions set the application route. Privacy rules on collecting candidate information from third parties changed in 2026. No NZ source found on Māori or Pacific norms specific to job outreach.

### Cited Findings
- Small market and networking: an employment site says building connections is critical in the smaller NZ job market, where personal introductions often lead to opportunities. Source: [Employment Hero NZ, "10 common job search mistakes to avoid"](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/common-mistakes-jobseekers-make/) (search excerpt; authority moderate).
- Public sector: most NZ government agencies advertise vacancies on jobs.govt.nz, and the Public Service Act 2020 requires advertising so suitably qualified applicants are aware of vacancies. Sources: [jobs.govt.nz](https://jobs.govt.nz/) and [Public Service Commissioner Guide for Applicants 2024 v3 (PDF)](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/assets/DirectoryFile/Public-Service-Commissioner-Guide-for-Applicants-2024-v3.pdf) (search results; the Guide was not read in full).
- Public sector portals vary by agency: the Ministry for the Environment, for example, sends formal offers via its own careers portal. Source: [MfE, "Guidance on applying for roles at MfE"](https://environment.govt.nz/about-us/jobs/guidance-on-applying-for-roles-at-mfe) (search excerpt).
- A 2026 Te Kawa Mataaho graduate listing asked applicants to state their preferred intake in the application. Source: [jobs.govt.nz, NZ 19085](https://jobs.govt.nz/jobs/NZ-19085) (search excerpt; illustrates listing-specific instructions, not a general rule).
- Public sector recruitment expects declared conflicts of interest and runs checks on the preferred candidate only. Source: search excerpts from agency guidance (see the Guide above).
- Privacy: from 1 May 2026, IPP 3A (added by the Privacy Amendment Act 2025) requires employers to tell candidates when information is being collected about them from third parties, what it is, and how it will be used. Sources: employer and law-firm notices, e.g. [Lane Neave, "New Information Privacy Principle"](https://www.laneneave.co.nz/news-events/privacy-principle/) and [Employment Hero, "Privacy Act IPP 3A"](https://employmenthero.com/nz/resources/privacy-act-ipp-3a-reference-checks/). CONFLICT: an earlier law-firm note gives a 1 June 2025 start date, written while the bill was pending; the 2026 date is used here. The statutory text was not checked.
- Māori unemployment was 6.3% in 2022 against 3.0% for New Zealand Europeans, per HR magazine. Source: [HRNZ/HR Magazine, "Tikanga at work" article](https://www.hcamag.com/nz/specialisation/diversity-inclusion/tikanga-at-work-cultural-competence-in-2025/547751) (search excerpt; 2022 figure, dated).
- Randstad NZ (2024): 35% of employees from minority groups including Māori and Pasifika report identity-based barriers to progression. Source: [NZ Herald, Maori and Pasifika job barriers article](https://nzherald.co.nz/kahu/maori-and-pasifika-face-job-barriers-despite-new-zealands-diversity-push/DITUVZJGBJC3RNXZA5QA43PZX4) (search excerpt; cited figure as reported, not checked against the Randstad report).

### Inferences
- In a public-sector application, direct outreach to hiring managers during the open process is likely unwelcome or inappropriate; the portal and the listing's instructions govern. Stated as inference, not as a rule any source found.
- Under IPP 3A, an app that gathers information about a hiring manager or HR contact from third-party sources should be designed so the employer's disclosure duties are not bypassed by the candidate's tooling. This is an inference for the design, not legal advice.

### Gaps
- No NZ source found on Māori or Pacific norms for approaching employers or for interview and outreach etiquette. The only found material was a dated, non-authoritative training page, which was not relied on.
- No NZ source found on whether hiring managers treat outreach differently across sectors beyond the public-sector portal point.
- The Ministry of Pacific Peoples or Te Puni Kōkiri resources may hold relevant guidance; not checked.

## Limits of this research
- Several sources are search-result excerpts rather than fetched pages; verify SEEK and NZ Herald wording before quoting.
- Dated sources: Ask a Manager (2014), Hays hidden-market item (date unconfirmed), WikiEducator (not used for claims), Unitec PDF (undated).
- Robert Half and TEC figures are quoted as reported; not independently verified.
- Recommended for the app: treat hiring-contact discovery as a manual, user-led step; keep any suggested outreach to one message per application, after the stated wait; do not automate contact harvesting or personal-channel messaging.
