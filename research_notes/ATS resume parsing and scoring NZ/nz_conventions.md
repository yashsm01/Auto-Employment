# New Zealand CV Conventions (2025-2026)

Scope: what NZ employers, recruiters and government career services expect in a CV, including notes for migrants and visa holders. Australia and UK differences are noted only where a source mentioned them.

Source-currency note: careers.govt.nz is decommissioned and redirects to Tahatu (see the final section). Most primary guidance below is from tahatu.govt.nz, Hays NZ, Employment Hero NZ, and employment.govt.nz. Recruiter pages are opinion, not rules.

## 1. Length, section order and section names

### Takeaway
Target two pages. Two to three pages is accepted by some sources. Put the most relevant content on page one. Use the standard NZ section names listed below.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatu (NZ government career service, successor to careers.govt.nz): "keep your CV to 2 pages if you can"; "it's OK to keep your CV short." Suggested headings in order: Name and contact details; Personal statement; Key skills and experience; Work history; Education and qualifications; Interests (optional); Referees. — [Tahatu, How to write a CV](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Hays NZ: "Your resume should be approximately two pages long." Recommended order: contact details; professional summary (50 to 150 words); skills summary (bulleted, use keywords from the ad); achievements (quantified, with percentages and time periods); work experience (reverse chronological, the most detailed section); education and qualifications; references. — [Hays NZ, How to write a CV](https://www.hays.net.nz/career-advice/tips-for-writing-great-resumes-1682)
- Employment Hero NZ: "two pages should be enough to convey your suitability"; include roughly the last 10 years of work history, plus older roles that directly relate to the job. — [Employment Hero NZ, CV dos and don'ts](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Robert Half NZ: usually reverse chronological; keep to two to three pages. Search snippet only, not fetched. — [Robert Half NZ, resume include](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/insights/career-development/resume-include.html)
- Robert Walters NZ expert, quoted by Newshub (June 2024): "anywhere between two and four pages is probably about right"; key selling points on page one. Search snippet only, and older and more lenient than the others. — [Newshub, Robert Walters recruiter quote](https://newshub.co.nz/home/new-zealand/2024/06/secrets-to-a-successful-cv-recruitment-expert-on-why-you-should-be-making-videos-using-ai.html)
- Trade Me NZ: two or three pages. Search snippet only. — [Trade Me careers advice](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-write-a-great-new-zealand-cv-with-free-templates-)
- Tahatu on work history: include both month and year for start and end dates, because years alone "can raise questions about employment gaps." Explain gaps positively. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Tahatu formatting: plain black font (example given: Calibri 11); bullet points, short paragraphs, bold headings, white space; avoid images; keep formatting simple so applicant tracking systems can read it. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Employment Hero NZ: avoid heavy graphics; use simple fonts such as Arial or Calibri; leave out unrelated jobs and volunteer roles; leave out high school details unless it is your only qualification. — [Employment Hero NZ](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Tahatu: AI tools can "generate inaccurate skills, experiences, achievements and qualifications," so applicants must review and personalise output. — [Hays NZ](https://www.hays.net.nz/career-advice/tips-for-writing-great-resumes-1682) (the AI point is on the Hays page as fetched; treat as recruiter guidance)

### Inferences
- A builder should default to two pages, allow a third page for senior or long-career candidates, and order sections: contact, personal statement or profile, key skills, work history (reverse chronological), education and qualifications, achievements (can sit in work history), interests (optional), referees line.
- The term "Personal statement" is the Tahatu term; Hays uses "Professional summary". Either works; a builder can offer "Profile" as an alias.
- Achievement bullets should carry numbers and dates where possible (Hays guidance).

### Gaps
- No primary-source guidance found on whether "Achievements" should be a standalone section or folded into work history. Sources differ.
- Robert Walters and Robert Half figures are search snippets or 2024 quotes, not fetched primary pages.

## 2. Referees

### Takeaway
Default NZ practice is a closing line, "Referees available on request", with names and contact details supplied when invited to interview. Include named referees only if the job ad asks for them.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatu: include referees "if the job ad asks for them" and then give contact details for "at least 2." Otherwise write "Referees available on request" and give names when invited to interview. Prepare 2 to 3 referees. Choose people who can speak to your work, such as a current or former manager, or a teacher or coach if your experience is limited. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Careers NZ content (via search snippet of a careers.govt.nz page, now redirected): one referee should be your current manager, team leader or work experience supervisor. — [Careers NZ, parts of a CV (search snippet)](https://www.careers.govt.nz/job-hunting/cvs-and-cover-letters/parts-of-a-cv)
- Hays: "References are available upon request" is common; referees ideally should be people you reported to directly. — [Hays NZ](https://www.hays.net.nz/career-advice/tips-for-writing-great-resumes-1682)
- Employment Hero NZ: do not list referees directly; add the closing line "References available on request." — [Employment Hero NZ](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Unitec guidelines (search snippet): if you write the on-request line, you can leave contact details off the CV, but ask your referees first. — [Unitec CV guidelines (PDF, search snippet)](https://www.unitec.ac.nz/sites/default/files/public/documents/CV%20Guidelines.pdf)
- Consent: employment.govt.nz says employers "must not collect information about them from someone who is not their nominated referee" without consent. — [Employment NZ, discrimination when hiring / hiring process](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/the-hiring-process)
- Dissent: an NMIT template (search snippet) advises against "available on request" as a phrase. SEEK NZ argues the phrase is so common it has become largely irrelevant. — [SEEK NZ, references available upon request (search snippet)](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/what-does-references-available-upon-request-mean)

### Inferences
- Builder default: "Referees available on request" as the last line. Offer an optional referees block (two or three names, role, relationship, phone or email) only when the job ad asks for it.
- Migrants with little NZ work history: advice is to include a local referee if they have any NZ work, and a former manager or teacher otherwise. Confirm referee consent before listing anyone.

### Gaps
- No NZ-specific rule on whether referees must be contactable by email or phone. Sources only say include contact details when asked.

## 3. Personal details: photo, DOB, gender, marital status, nationality, address

### Takeaway
Do not include photo, date of birth, marital status, gender, or nationality. Name, city or suburb, phone, professional email and optional LinkedIn or portfolio link are the expected personal details. Human Rights Act 1993 makes questions about these grounds risky for employers, so most NZ guides advise applicants to leave them off.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatu: "Put your name and contact details at the top"; use a professional email, ideally including your name; links to LinkedIn or a professional site are fine. The page gives "no advice on other personal details, such as age or marital status." It also says "don't use images." — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Hays NZ: contact details are name, phone, professional-sounding email, and LinkedIn; use the phone and email you check most often. No guidance on photo, DOB, or marital status. — [Hays NZ](https://www.hays.net.nz/career-advice/tips-for-writing-great-resumes-1682)
- Employment NZ, discrimination when hiring: employers must "make sure you do not unlawfully [discriminate] based on sex, marital or family status, age, religious or ethical belief or any other prohibited ground." — [Employment NZ, discrimination when hiring](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/discrimination-when-hiring/)
- Human Rights Act 1993 applies to the whole hiring process, including job advertisements, application forms, interviews and job offers. It is unlawful to ask a job applicant questions that show an intention to discriminate on a prohibited ground. Search summaries of HRC pre-employment guidance; not fetched. — [Human Rights Act 1993 (legislation.govt.nz)](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/82/en/latest/)
- Age: the Act's age ground covers people aged 16 and over (search summary of a law firm page). Auckland University guidance says applicants do not have to give their age in a job application or interview. — [University of Auckland, age discrimination](https://auckland.ac.nz/en/about-us/about-the-university/equity-at-the-university/equity-information-for-staff/mature-age-staff/information-for-mature-age-staff/age-discrimination.html)
- Privacy Act and screening: pre-employment screening "must be relevant to the job and/or workplace"; employers must check the applicant's legal right to work. — [Employment NZ, the hiring process](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/the-hiring-process)
- Business associations say pre-employment screening must stay within both the Privacy Act 2020 and the Human Rights Act (search summary of a business-association A-Z guide). — [Business Central A-Z guide (PDF, search summary)](https://businesscentral.org.nz/sites/default/files/2025-03/A-Z%20Guide%20-%20Application%20for%20employment.pdf)
- Public Service Census (for public agencies) is voluntary and confidential, with a prefer-not-to-answer option; ethnicity data for the public service comes from that census, not from applications (search summary, publicservice.govt.nz). — [Public Service Commission, ethnicity data](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/data/workforce-data/diversity/ethnicity)

### Inferences
- A NZ-style CV should not show photo, DOB, age, marital or family status, gender, religion, or nationality. The reasons are the Human Rights Act grounds (age, sex, marital or family status, religious or ethical belief, race or ethnicity, disability) and the fact that employers should not ask for them at application stage.
- Suburb or city is enough for address. Full postal address is not expected; this is an inference, since no source said so either way.
- Nationality is not the expected detail. Work rights status is the employment-relevant item (section 4).
- A builder should warn users that a photo can signal protected grounds such as age, race or sex, so it is discouraged.

### Gaps
- Photo guidance: no NZ source found that directly addresses photos on CVs. Tahatu's "don't use images" is the closest, and it is about formatting. The HRC pre-employment guide may address photos but could not be fetched.
- Privacy Act Information Privacy Principles: the primary page on privacy.org.nz could not be fetched (HTTP 404). The principle wording is not verified in this note. Verify before quoting it in product copy.
- The Human Rights Commission A-Z pre-employment guide (Getting a Job) could not be fetched; the grounds list here comes from employment.govt.nz and search summaries.
- Date of birth: no source directly bans DOB on a CV; the age-question rule implies it should be left off.

## 4. Visa and work-rights statements

### Takeaway
There is no official NZ format for a work-rights line on a CV. Practice is a short status line near the top, such as "NZ citizen", "NZ resident", or the visa type with its expiry date and any employer or role limits. Recruiters will verify status, so accuracy matters more than wording.

### Cited Findings
- Employers "must check that a job applicant has the legal right to work in New Zealand before you employ them." — [Employment NZ, the hiring process](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/the-hiring-process)
- Checks are made through Immigration NZ VisaView (with the person's consent) or by document check. Accepted documents include a foreign passport with a valid NZ work visa, an Australian passport, and a Refugee Travel Document with a valid work visa. Some visas are restricted to a specific employer or type of work. — [Immigration NZ, check if someone can legally work for you](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/for-employers/hiring-people-from-overseas/check-if-someone-can-legally-work-for-you/)
- Who can work: NZ and Australian citizens, holders of an NZ residence visa, holders of an NZ work visa, or temporary-visa holders whose conditions allow work. — [Employment NZ, right to work in New Zealand](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/right-to-work-in-new-zealand/) (search summary; not fetched)
- Penalty: a search summary reports an infringement fee of NZD 1,000 for individuals and NZD 3,000 for companies for hiring someone not entitled to work. Not verified on a primary page. — [Immigration NZ, check if someone can legally work for you](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/work/for-employers/hiring-people-from-overseas/check-if-someone-can-legally-work-for-you/)
- MBIE recruiter guide: recruiters have a duty of care to ensure clients follow ethical and sustainable work practices, and include due-diligence questions on whether an employer has employed migrants who were not allowed to work. — [MBIE recruiters guide (search summary)](https://www.employment.govt.nz/assets/uploads/documents/fair-work-practices/ethical-and-sustainable-work-practices/recruiters-guide.pdf)
- Immigration NZ "Make your overseas experience relevant to New Zealand" (search summary): explain in an interview why overseas experience is valuable to the employer. — [Immigration NZ / Live Work, make your overseas experience relevant](https://www.live-work.immigration.govt.nz/work-in-new-zealand/finding-applying-for-jobs/succeeding-in-job-interviews/make-your-overseas-experience-relevant-to-new)

### Inferences
- Recommended builder line (inferred, not sourced as official): "Work rights: NZ citizen" / "Work rights: NZ resident" / "Work rights: Open work visa, valid to [month year]". Add "employer-specific" only if the visa is restricted.
- The user's wording "Open work visa" is a real visa category name, but the line should state the actual visa type and expiry date from the grant notice, not a general description.
- Recruiters are obliged to check, so a vague or missing status line can cost a shortlist place. A clear statement at the top of page one is the practical approach.
- Do not claim eligibility beyond the visa conditions; the employer checks through VisaView.

### Gaps
- No NZ source gives a template or required wording for a work-rights statement on a CV.
- Immigration NZ live-work pages were redirected and not fetched in full. Visa-type lists and current document rules should be re-checked on immigration.govt.nz before the builder hard-codes them.

## 5. Spelling, date formats, phone formats, CV vs resume

### Takeaway
Use NZ/British spelling (for example "organisation", "programme", "personalise"). Write dates as month and year. Use "CV" as the document name, and name the file "First-Last-CV.pdf". Phone format is not specified by any source found.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatu uses "CV" in its own title and body. Its file advice: send a PDF "unless the employer asks for a Word document." Name the file with first and last name and "CV", for example "sam-henderson-CV.pdf". — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Tahatu date rule: month and year for start and finish dates. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Hays: "The terms are now used interchangeably" (CV and resume); the term "has no bearing on your chance of reaching an interview shortlist." — [Hays NZ](https://www.hays.net.nz/career-advice/tips-for-writing-great-resumes-1682)
- Tahatu on proofreading: "proofread it carefully and ask someone else to check it too." No spelling variant specified. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)

### Inferences
- Spelling: NZ English (British-based), for example "organisation", "prioritise", "licence" (noun), "programme". Not sourced by a style guide in this research; based on NZ government usage in the fetched Tahatu page.
- Dates: "Mar 2021 - Present" or "03/2021". Avoid US "MM/DD/YYYY". The NZ government form convention is day/month/year, so the builder should never default to US-style dates in free text.
- Phone: NZ mobile numbers are typically written "021 123 4567" or "+64 21 123 4567". This is an inference from common usage, not a source. Mark as inferred in the UI.
- Name the document "CV", not "resume", for NZ employers.

### Gaps
- No source found giving NZ phone format or NZ spelling rules as an authority. Treat the phone format and spelling list above as inference.

## 6. Cover letter and public-sector specifics

### Takeaway
A cover letter is expected with the CV, and it should explain gaps and relocation. Public-sector roles use their own application forms and selection criteria. Ethnicity and diversity data is collected separately, not on the application.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatu: "Send your CV with a cover letter. Together they make your first impression." Explain large gaps in work history in the cover letter. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Employment Hero NZ: cover letters should be one to two pages. — [Employment Hero NZ](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Public Service Commission: ethnicity data is gathered through the voluntary Public Service Census, which includes a prefer-not-to-answer option; the census, not job applications, is the data source (search summary). — [Public Service Commission, ethnicity](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/data/workforce-data/diversity/ethnicity)
- A 2024 Public Service Commissioner application form (search summary of a .docx) asks about referees, conflicts of interest and prior convictions. No ethnicity field appeared in the excerpt. Serious-misconduct checks happen at the preferred-candidate stage. — [Public Service Commissioner application form 2024 (.docx)](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/assets/Application-Form-Public-Service-Commissioner-2024.docx)
- Public Service Commission recruitment guidance on eliminating bias and discrimination exists (PDF); it could not be read because the PDF text layer was not extractable. — [PSC guidance PDF](https://www.publicservice.govt.nz/assets/DirectoryFile/Guidance-eliminating-bias-and-discrimination-in-recruitment.pdf)
- FYI.org.nz OIA response (search summary): several public-sector organisations said they do not ask for ethnicity and gender in applications, or that it is self-identified. Practice varies by agency. — [FYI.org.nz OIA response](https://fyi.org.nz/request/12745/response/48732/attach/html/2/OIA%2026982%20Tania%20Te%20Whenua%20Response%20Letter.pdf.html)

### Inferences
- For public-sector jobs, the builder should support a separate output mode: a full CV plus a written response to each selection criterion (STAR-style examples), not a CV alone. This is an inference; the criteria format was not researched.
- Do not put ethnicity, gender or disability on a general CV. Where an agency asks, treat it as a separate optional field.

### Gaps
- Selection-criteria format and NZ Government Jobs (apply.govt.nz or agency portal) requirements were not researched.
- Whether a standard public-service application asks ethnicity is not confirmed; the one form found did not have it.
- Cover letter length and structure from a primary NZ government source were not found.

## 7. Common mistakes NZ recruiters flag for overseas-trained applicants

### Takeaway
The most-cited problems are a one-page CV, unfamiliar employers and qualifications with no context, no NZ work history or local referee, and dates without months. These come from recruiter blogs and government migrant guidance rather than formal rules.

### Cited Findings
- A one-page UK or US-style CV reads as thin by NZ norms; two to three pages is the common expectation (search summary of migrant-advice sites; recruiter opinion). — [Going NZ, How to write a NZ-style CV](https://goingnz.com/?p=1787)
- Local employers may not recognise overseas employers, job titles or qualifications. Add a short description of each organisation (size, location, what it does) and avoid acronyms that are not explained. (search summary) — [Going NZ, NZ-style CV](https://goingnz.com/?p=1787)
- Lack of NZ experience is widely seen as the biggest hurdle; short NZ-based work can provide a local reference. (search summary) — [Immigration NZ, value overseas experience](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/employ-migrants/explore-your-options/finding-and-hiring-workers-overseas/interviewing-candidates-job-offer/value-overseas-experience)
- Immigration NZ guidance: employers want relevant information only, not every job or course. — [Immigration NZ, value overseas experience](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/employ-migrants/explore-your-options/finding-and-hiring-workers-overseas/interviewing-candidates-job-offer/value-overseas-experience)
- Tahatu: unexplained year-only dates raise questions about gaps; use month and year. — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)
- Employment Hero NZ: do not exaggerate achievements; be honest about responsibilities and results. — [Employment Hero NZ](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Tahatu: avoid overused CV cliches; use strong action verbs such as "managed" or "delivered." — [Tahatu](https://www.tahatu.govt.nz/work/applying-for-a-job/how-to-write-a-cv)

### Inferences
- The builder should flag: one-page CVs, overseas employers with no context, unexplained acronyms, year-only dates, and no NZ referee or NZ-experience section when the candidate is new to NZ.
- Suggest a short "NZ context" line for each overseas employer (industry, size, country) without adding unverifiable claims.
- Warn against claims of NZ experience or qualification equivalence that the user cannot evidence. Qualification recognition (NZQA) was not researched in this note.

### Gaps
- No primary source on NZQA equivalence for overseas qualifications, which many migrant CVs need to address. Not researched.
- Most "mistake" lists are recruiter blog content. Treat as opinion.

## Source currency and flags

### Takeaway
Use tahatu.govt.nz, not careers.govt.nz. Several older-dated sources are in use. Re-check any visa or penalty figure against immigration.govt.nz before shipping.

### Cited Findings
- careers.govt.nz redirects (301) to the Tahatu homepage with a decommission campaign tag. Its "tips for creating a NZ-style CV" page is no longer served there. — [careers.govt.nz (redirect)](https://careers.govt.nz/job-hunting/new-to-new-zealand/tips-for-creating-a-nz-style-cv)
- A careers.govt.nz notice (search snippet) says its CV Builder was not available after 18 September 2025. — [Knowyourcv careers.govt.nz (search snippet)](https://knowyourcv.careers.govt.nz/job-hunting/new-to-new-zealand/tips-for-creating-a-nz-style-cv)
- Immigration NZ live-work pages redirect to immigration.govt.nz pages; the NZ-style CV page moved and was not fetched. — [Immigration NZ](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/live/setting-up-your-life-in-new-zealand/)
- Employment Hero NZ CV article: published 1 August 2025, shown as updated 15 September 2026 on the fetched page. — [Employment Hero NZ](https://employmenthero.com/nz/blog/cv-dos-and-donts/)
- Hays NZ page has no publication date shown. Robert Walters quote is from June 2024. Tahatu page date not shown in fetched text.
- Search results for HRC pre-employment material are mostly from business associations and law firms.

### Inferences
- Treat the Tahatu page as the current government baseline for CV content.
- Recruiter sources (Hays, Employment Hero, Robert Half, Robert Walters, Trade Me) are opinion and mostly agree on two pages, reverse chronology, no photo, referees on request.

### Gaps
- Human Rights Commission pre-employment guide (A-Z) not fetched.
- Privacy Act IPPs not verified from a primary page.
- NZ Government Jobs selection-criteria requirements not researched.
- NZQA qualification recognition not researched.
- Phone and spelling rules not found in an authoritative source.
