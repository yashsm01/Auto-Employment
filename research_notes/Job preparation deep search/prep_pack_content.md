# Per-Job Preparation Pack: NZ Software/IT Roles (Application and Interview)

Scope: one job at one employer, New Zealand, software/IT roles. Sources are mostly 2025-2026. Items marked [general] are not NZ-specific. Items marked [snippet] came from search-result summaries and were not fully opened. Items marked [aggregator] are third-party summaries, not primary sources.

Source-quality flags:
- The careers.govt.nz site is being decommissioned. Its redirect points to the Tahatū Career Navigator site (tahatu.govt.nz). Use Tahatū links, not careers.govt.nz links.
- The Hays salary page returned no role-level NZ IT figures.
- The Robert Walters salary-expectations 2026 page and the Hays and Absolute IT salary pages returned HTTP 403 or redirects, so their figures are from search snippets.

## Key Question 1: What do NZ recruiters, hiring managers and careers services say to research before applying and before an interview?

### Takeaway
Every NZ source converges on the same core: research the organisation's needs and current situation, re-read the job description and map your evidence to each requirement, and prepare STAR-style examples and questions to ask. None of the sources give NZ-specific detail on how to research a company's culture, referees or work rights. Those are gaps.

### Cited Findings
- Research the organisation to understand what it wants in an employee; check its website for financial information and current situation; look up the interviewer — [Trade Me/Careers advice guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz) [aggregator; URL seen in search results, content only partly opened]; [SEEK NZ guide](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/article/how-to-prepare-for-your-interview-the-ultimate-guide) [snippet]
- Tahatū tips (current NZ government careers site): research the organisation's culture and what it wants in an employee; use your research to answer "Why do you want to work for us?"; use research to draft questions; bring examples of your work; review your application to recall skills; prepare examples showing how you used skills; practise out loud; check whether you need to prepare for tests, a presentation, or group activities — [Tahatū tips for interviews](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews)
- Tahatū page gives no specific tips on researching the interviewer, and no NZ-specific guidance on culture, references or work rights — [Tahatū tips for interviews](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews)
- Re-read the advertisement and position description, list the key skills, and for each requirement you do not yet meet prepare a response — [Tahatū search results summary](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews) [snippet]; [Robert Half NZ interview guide](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/insights/landing-job/interview) [snippet]
- Ask the employer what the interview format will be before going in, since NZ interview formats vary by job and employer — [SEEK/Employment NZ search summary](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/interviews/interview-tips-for-applicants/) [snippet]
- Use STAR for behavioural questions — [Employment NZ / Careers NZ search summary](https://www.employment.govt.nz/starting-employment/hiring/interviews/interview-tips-for-applicants/) [snippet]
- Arrive about 10-15 minutes early; plan the route and parking; test video tech and lighting for video interviews — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz); [Tahatū tips](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews). The Trade Me guide warns that "even a short walk in a Wellington southerly can undo a carefully planned outfit."
- Do not raise salary or leave entitlements in a first interview unless the interviewer raises them first — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)
- Gaps: for a skills gap, point to equivalent experience or briefly acknowledge the gap and explain how you are closing it. This guidance covers skills gaps only, not employment gaps — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)
- Dress: office roles default to smart casual, "err on the side of slightly more formal" — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)
- Company research checklist for tech roles (mission/values from About page, recent news, main products, culture via LinkedIn/Glassdoor, competitors without criticising them) — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz) [aggregator/general]

### Inferences
- Core research for a pack, in priority order: (1) the job description and the employer's stated must-haves; (2) recent employer news, product, funding or restructure signals; (3) the team and hiring manager's public footprint (LinkedIn, conference talks, GitHub, blog); (4) the employer's stated culture and values.
- Items (1) and (2) can be gathered by an agent. Item (3) can be partly gathered, but a candidate must check for accuracy and privacy. Item (4) needs candidate judgement because published values are marketing.
- Questions to ask the interviewer are a high-value section because the Tahatū guidance explicitly frames the interview as the candidate's chance to decide whether the job suits them.

### Gaps
- No NZ-specific guide found on how to research a company's culture, or whether employee reviews such as Glassdoor are commonly used by NZ hiring managers.
- Careers.govt.nz 2024-2026 pages on employer research were not fully fetched. The Tahatū pages are the current source.
- The Trade Me and SEEK guides were only partly read.

## Key Question 2: What interview formats and question types are common for software/IT roles in New Zealand, and are there NZ-specific norms?

### Takeaway
The evidence is anecdotal. Take-home tasks, technical screens (Codility-style tests) and pair programming sessions all appear in NZ tech interview reports. There is no NZ-wide survey of which format is most common. NZ norms on culture fit, te reo and tikanga, work rights and referee checks are barely covered by the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- A typical process described by a general NZ guide: recruiter phone screen (15-30 min), technical interview or coding challenge, culture-fit interview, final chat with senior leader. Some companies move in 2-3 weeks; others take 6-8 weeks, especially government roles. [aggregator: Employment Hero / jobrise NZ guide, snippet] — [Jobrise NZ software engineer guide](https://jobrise.io/en/blog/new-zealand-software-engineer-application-guide-2026/)
- Take-home assignments: a candidate reported a take-home API client (Go) for a NZ fintech; another reported a six-hour take-home followed by an on-site technical interview [individual Glassdoor reports, anecdotal] — [Glassdoor Form3 interview reviews](https://www.glassdoor.co.nz/Interview/Form3-Interview-E2008415-RVW53132107.htm)
- Timed take-home test: Pushpay candidates reported a coding test to complete within four hours [anecdotal, date uncertain] — [Glassdoor Pushpay interview review](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Pushpay-Interview-E1021369-RVW62333977.htm)
- Automated tests: FNZ reviews mention a Codility test with easy to lower-intermediate questions, plus an SQL task [anecdotal] — [Glassdoor FNZ interview reviews](https://www.glassdoor.co.nz/Interview/FNZ-Interview-E458401-RVW97803389.htm) (URL seen in search; content only partly confirmed)
- Pair programming and whiteboarding: a UK-based consultancy with NZ roles reported 25-30 minutes of system whiteboarding then 25-30 minutes of pair programming with TDD [UK-based, anecdotal] — [Glassdoor Made Tech review](https://www.glassdoor.co.nz/Interview/Made-Tech-Interview-E1330562-RVW99862765.htm)
- AI-led first-round screening is now used in NZ. Employment Hero reported over 2,500 AI-led interviews in one month (April 2026, single company). A 1News reporter's trial (May 2026) found a roughly five-minute AI call with an AI voice that generated follow-up prompts, with no human on the line. [1News, 21 May 2026](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/21/ai-is-interviewing-thousands-of-kiwi-job-seekers-so-i-gave-it-a-try/); Employment Hero CEO quoted: "one of our firm rules is that there's always a human in the loop."
- Video and phone interview guidance exists on Tahatū — [Tahatū video and phone interviews](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/video-and-phone-interviews) [URL from search; not fetched]
- The Tahatū "tests at interviews" page covers the types of tests used in NZ job interviews — [Tahatū tests at interviews](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tests-at-interviews) [URL from search; not fetched]

### Inferences
- A pack for an NZ software role should list the likely stages and the likely format of each, with a note that the format is unconfirmed until the recruiter says so.
- Most-likely question types, based on the sources: behavioural (STAR), technical depth on the stack in the job ad, a practical task (take-home, live coding, or pair programming), and a culture or values conversation.
- For an AI-screened first round, a pack should advise practising short structured answers, because the screening is timed and follow-ups are generated live.

### Gaps
- No NZ-wide 2025-2026 survey of tech interview formats was found.
- No source found on te reo, tikanga, work-rights questions or referee checks in NZ tech hiring. Those are gaps to flag for the report writer and should not be filled from general knowledge.
- Glassdoor reports are individual, small-sample and undated in several cases.

## Key Question 3: What salary and pay-range information sources exist for NZ tech roles, how current are they, and how should a pack present a range?

### Takeaway
Several NZ sources publish 2025-2026 salary guides, but their methods differ (base vs total package, survey vs job-ad data, gross vs net, which regions). Absolute IT's current guide was not found, and no source gave a verified NZ software developer figure for Hamilton or Wellington. A pack should show a range built from two or more sources, label each one's method and date, and tie it to the role level and region.

### Cited Findings
- Hays NZ IT salary guide (FY26/27 edition, published 28 May 2026 per a search result): "more than 7,000 professionals, 26% of them in New Zealand"; NZ figures are base salaries only and exclude KiwiSaver employer contributions. The fetched page gives no role-level NZ IT figures, and it does not state the region or survey date for IT figures. Full guide is available by form. — [Hays IT salary guide page](https://www.hays.net.nz/it/it-salary-guide-new-zealand); [Hays press release FY26/27 (search snippet)](https://www.hays.net.nz/press-release/content/hays-salary-guide-fy26-27) — average increase 3.3% (search snippet)
- Robert Walters 2026 NZ/AU salary expectations (search snippet): market "stabilising, with moderate, targeted pay growth." IT Director range roughly NZ$150,000-320,000 depending on organisation size, industry and location. Role-level tech figures not verified. — [Robert Walters salary expectations 2026](https://www.robertwalters.co.nz/insights/hiring-advice/blog/salary-expectations-2026.html) [snippet; page returned 403 on fetch]
- Robert Half NZ 2026 technology salary tables: gross annual salaries, excluding bonuses, benefits and KiwiSaver (per the 2025 edition's note). — [Robert Half NZ technology salary guide](https://www.roberthalf.com/nz/en/insights/salary-guide/technology) [snippet]
- Absolute IT: only the 2025 Salary & Benefits Guide was found, as a listing page without figures. No 2026 edition found. — [Absolute IT search result] [gap]
- Payscale (aggregator, self-reported survey data), 2026 figures as shown in snippets: Christchurch software developer median NZ$71k, 25th-75th percentile NZ$57k-79k [aggregator]; Wellington page showed national figures (median NZ$76k), so no verified Wellington-specific median. — [Payscale Christchurch](https://www.payscale.com/research/NZ/Job=Software_Developer/Salary/db7295b5/Christchurch); [Payscale Wellington](https://www.payscale.com/research/NZ/Job=Software_Developer/Salary/79165fa1/Wellington)
- Indeed (job-ad-based, snippet): Wellington City developer about NZ$90,581 (5 salaries only); Wellington City software engineer NZ$133,512; Christchurch software engineer NZ$103,065; Albany software engineer NZ$103,614. Titles are not like-for-like. — [Indeed NZ software engineer salaries](https://nz.indeed.com/career/software-engineer/salaries) [snippet]
- SEEK's salary insights are built from salary ranges employers disclose on SEEK job ads; the Christchurch programmer page was found but the figures did not come through. — [SEEK programmer salary, Christchurch](https://nz.seek.com/career-advice/role/programmer/salary/in-christchurch) [snippet]
- Hamilton: only an undated, weak source (leverageedu.com, NZ$85,500 average). Treat as unverified. — [leverageedu](https://leverageedu.com/learn/?p=60552) [gap-level]
- Trade Me guide: salary questions are for later in the process. — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)

### Inferences
- Presenting a range: show the source, the date, the method (base vs total package, gross), the region and the role title. Give a band (for example a 25th-75th percentile range) rather than a single number, and show where the posted job-ad range falls within it.
- The best single number for a specific job is the employer's posted range, if any. Second best is SEEK's ad-disclosed range for the same title and town.
- Agents can reliably gather: posted ad ranges, Payscale and Indeed bands, and the publication dates of each guide. The candidate must decide where they sit in the range, based on their own experience, the total package (KiwiSaver, bonus, leave) and the cost of living in their town.
- Flag that the Hays and Robert Half figures are base or gross and exclude super and bonus.

### Gaps
- No verified NZ software developer median for Wellington, Hamilton or other regional towns from a primary salary guide.
- Hays and Robert Walters role-level NZ tech figures were not retrieved. Absolute IT 2026 figures were not found.
- The Hays page does not state its survey year for IT.

## Key Question 4: What do existing AI job-prep / interview-prep tools put in their per-job prep output? Which features do users value, and which do they complain about?

### Takeaway
The tools split into two groups. Trackers (Teal, Huntr) manage applications and tailor resumes. Interview coaches (Final Round AI and others) run mock interviews, question banks and, in some cases, live copilots. Per-job company research is not a clear feature of Final Round AI, based on the third-party review. Evidence on hallucinated facts and generic questions was not found in the sources. This is a major gap, and a pack that relies on AI output needs human checking.

### Cited Findings
- Teal and Huntr are trackers. Teal: free resume builder, AI resume rewrites, integrated job tracker. Huntr: one-click saving from job boards, favoured for high-volume applying. Pricing for Huntr Pro reported at about $30-50/mo, and both have free tiers. — [Rework roundup](https://resources.rework.com/tools/ai-tools/best-ai-tools-for-job-seekers-2026) [aggregator]; [Huntr vs Teal, Huntr's own blog](https://huntr.co/blog/huntr-vs-teal) [vendor-biased]
- Final Round AI: described as a full interview-prep suite with resume writing, mock interviews, LinkedIn optimisation and real-time in-interview coaching. Reviewers say the post-interview analytics may be more valuable long-term than live suggestions. Pricing reported differently by sources, about $90-96/mo monthly billing; check the current price. — [Final Round AI review, DEV Community](https://dev.to/finalroundai/i-reviewed-final-round-ai-for-technical-interviews-heres-what-actually-matters-in-2026-47gd) [vendor-authored]; [Final Round summary, Rework roundup](https://resources.rework.com/tools/ai-tools/best-ai-tools-for-job-seekers-2026) [aggregator]
- Final Round AI feature list from a competing product's review (LoopCV): AI copilot with live transcription and suggested answers, AI mock interviews with feedback, large question bank, role- and industry-specific practice, coding support depending on plan, post-interview feedback. The review does not list per-job prep or company research. Reported costs of about $96/mo entry and $148/mo higher tier. Complaints it cites (paraphrased from a Reddit user and a Trustpilot reviewer): the price is steep for a few weeks of use; nervousness about relying on the live copilot. It flags live in-interview assistance as an ethical grey area that may be detectable. — [LoopCV Final Round review](https://www.loopcv.pro/directory/finalround/) [competitor review, not independent]
- Employment Hero's AI recruitment assistant (employer-side) is an NZ example of AI in hiring, not a candidate tool. Its CEO acknowledged bias risk. — [1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/05/21/ai-is-interviewing-thousands-of-kiwi-job-seekers-so-i-gave-it-a-try/)
- Unitec runs an AI interview coach for its students: students upload a CV and job description and receive tailored questions. — [Unitec, search snippet](https://www.unitec.ac.nz/ai-interview-coach-gives-akonga-a-safe-space-to-build-confidence/) [snippet]
- Overall reviewer consensus (aggregators) is that no single tool covers everything; users combine a tracker with an interview coach. — [Rework roundup](https://resources.rework.com/tools/ai-tools/best-ai-tools-for-job-seekers-2026) [aggregator]

### Inferences
- The valuable per-job sections in these tools are: tailored resume edits, a role-specific question bank, mock interview practice with feedback, and tracking. Per-job company research and salary range are less clearly offered and should be generated by the pack builder itself.
- The complaints found (cost, reliance on live copilots, ethics and detectability) suggest that a pack should avoid live in-interview assistance and focus on prep content.
- The lack of evidence on hallucination and generic questions is itself a finding. The pack builder should treat AI-generated company facts as unverified until the candidate checks them against a primary source.

### Gaps
- Not researched: Google Interview Warmup, Big Interview, Jobscan, and newer 2025-2026 per-job prep tools. These should be checked before drawing conclusions about the market.
- No evidence was found on hallucinated facts or generic questions. The Reddit r/newzealand and r/cscareerquestions searches returned no Reddit threads, so user-community opinion is missing.
- Final Round AI and Teal data come from aggregators and vendor-authored pages. Prices differ between sources.

## Key Question 5: For candidates in smaller regional NZ towns, what extra practical details matter?

### Takeaway
Sources on regional NZ job prep are thin. The Trade Me guide and Tahatū mention travel planning and the time needed for the trip. The sources do not cover remote or hybrid policies, relocation support or the local employer landscape. A pack for a regional role should cover commute, the remote option, and relocation and cost-of-living factors, but those points need candidate-specific research.

### Cited Findings
- Tahatū: work out how you will get there and how long it takes; have a back-up plan — [Tahatū tips for interviews](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews)
- Video interviews are common for remote-first candidates; test tech and lighting — [Tahatū tips](https://tahatu.govt.nz/work/doing-an-interview/tips-for-interviews); [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)
- Trade Me guide: plan the route, check roadworks, public transport delays and parking; allow 10-15 minutes early — [Trade Me guide](https://www.trademe.co.nz/c/jobs/article/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview-nz)
- Regional salary figures are the weakest part of the evidence. Payscale's Christchurch figure and Indeed's Wellington and Christchurch figures are the only regional data found (see Key Question 3).

### Inferences
- A regional pack should include: commute time and cost by car or bus at the interview hour; whether the role is on-site, hybrid or remote and whether the employer has said so; whether relocation support is offered; and whether the regional salary is adjusted for cost of living.
- Remote-friendly and hybrid roles are common targets for regional candidates. The pack should ask the employer directly in the first contact.
- Local employer landscape (which firms have offices in the town, and who is hiring) is a good agent task, but the sources found do not give a reliable NZ list.

### Gaps
- No NZ source on remote or hybrid tech hiring rates outside the main centres was found.
- No source on relocation packages or regional cost-of-living effects on tech salaries was found.
- Local employer landscape was not researched.

## Key Question 6 (Synthesis): Ranked pack sections and automation split

This is the synthesis for the report writer. It is an inference from the findings above, not a sourced ranking.

### Takeaway
A useful per-job pack has about eight sections. The highest-value ones are the job-to-evidence map, the likely interview format with prep for each stage, the pay band with its sources, and the employer and interviewer research. An agent can reliably gather the posted job details, the pay sources and the publication dates. Judgement is needed for the evidence map, the pay position, the salary ask, and questions about culture.

### Cited Findings
- Core interview preparation items drawn from the sources above: employer research, job-description mapping, STAR examples, questions to ask, format confirmation, logistics, dress, arrival time, and salary timing — see Key Questions 1-3 for sources.

### Inferences (ranked by likely impact on getting an interview and doing well in it)
1. Job-to-evidence map: each must-have and nice-to-have in the job ad, paired with a specific example from the candidate's history (STAR). Strongest impact on screening and interview. Agent can list requirements; candidate supplies the evidence.
2. Interview stages and format prep: the likely stages (recruiter screen, possible AI screen, technical or take-home, pair programming, culture or values, final panel), with a note that the format must be confirmed. Agent can draft from employer and sector sources; candidate should confirm with recruiter.
3. Pay band and negotiation position: a range with source, date, method, role and region (see Key Question 3). Agent can gather posted ranges and bands; candidate decides the target and the minimum.
4. Employer and team research: recent news, products, funding or restructure; what the team builds; the hiring manager's public footprint (privacy-limited). Agent can gather public facts with links. Candidate must judge which facts to use and check them.
5. Questions to ask the interviewer: drafted from the research, tailored to the team and stack. Agent can draft; candidate chooses.
6. Technical prep: the stack in the job ad, likely practical task types, and a practice plan. Agent can draft a study list; candidate must assess their own level.
7. Logistics and format: location, commute, arrival time, video set-up, and remote or hybrid status. Agent can check route and public transport; candidate decides.
8. Application tailoring: resume and cover-letter edits against the job ad. Agent can draft; candidate must check every claim. Hallucination risk is high here.

Automation split:
- Reliable for an agent: posted job details and requirements, employer public facts with links, salary sources with dates and methods, route and logistics, and a draft question list.
- Needs candidate judgement: which evidence to use, how to position on pay, whether to raise salary or leave, how to answer culture or values questions, and how to handle employment gaps. Also: whether to use AI tools live in an interview (the sources flag this as a risk).
- Needs verification even when an agent produces it: any employer claim (culture, growth, funding), any named interviewer, any pay figure, and any NZ norm the sources did not cover (te reo, tikanga, work rights, referees).

### Gaps
- No primary-source evidence on which section most improves interview rate. The ranking is inferred from the qualitative sources above and should be labelled as inference in the report.
- NZ norms on culture fit, te reo and tikanga, work rights and referee checks are not covered by any source found.
- Not verified: 2025-2026 NZ-specific interview format surveys, and regional remote-hiring data.
- Reddit r/newzealand and r/cscareerquestions were not reachable through search.
- Some sources were older (for example, the Absolute IT 2025 guide, the Robert Walters 2025 RWANZ guide, and undated Glassdoor reviews) and are flagged here.
